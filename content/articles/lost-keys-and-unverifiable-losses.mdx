An unspent output on the chain records two things: an amount, and a script
setting the condition under which it may be spent. That is the whole record.
There is no field for whether anybody still holds the key that satisfies the
script, no field for intent, and no field that distinguishes a person who cannot
spend from a person who has chosen not to.

Every published figure for how much has been permanently lost is an attempt to
recover a fact that the data structure does not contain.

## The one class of loss that is genuinely provable

Start with what can be established, because it is a real category and it is
smaller than people expect.

Some outputs are unspendable by construction. A script that can never evaluate
true, whatever is supplied to it, locks its amount away in a way that anybody can
verify by reading the script. An output paid to a script that begins with an
operation causing immediate failure is the clearest example, and the reference
implementation does not even carry such outputs in its set of spendable outputs,
because there is no circumstance in which it would need them.

There is also a documented structural case: the coinbase output of the very first
block is not spendable through the reference implementation, because that block is
not present in the transaction database the way every later block is. Whether that
was intended is not something the code settles, and this piece will not guess.

That is the honest boundary of the provable category. Scripts that cannot be
satisfied. Everything past that line is inference.

## What dormancy actually measures

The analytical tools people reach for measure age, not loss.

A dormancy metric asks how long an output has sat unmoved. Coin days destroyed
weights each moved amount by how long it had been still, so that old holdings
moving registers more strongly than recent ones. Age band charts group the
outstanding supply by time since last movement. All of these are computed from
the chain and all of them are correct as far as they go.

What they measure is the absence of a transaction. The set of outputs that have
not moved in a decade is a fact, and it can be recomputed by anybody. But absence
of movement has at least four explanations that the chain cannot tell apart: the
key was destroyed, the key was never recoverable in the first place, the holder is
deliberately not moving it, or the holder is dead or otherwise unreachable while
the key still exists somewhere. Three of those four are recoverable positions and
one is not, and they produce identical evidence.

There is a further trap in the same place. Movement is not proof of possession
either. Custodial services consolidate and reshuffle holdings for their own
operational reasons, and a single moving output may correspond to any number of
underlying account holders, or to none at all. So the metric is noisy in both
directions: stillness does not mean loss, and motion does not mean an owner
decided anything.

## The estimates are models wearing the clothes of measurements

Here is the shape of essentially every loss figure in circulation. Take the set
of outputs that have not moved since before some cut off. Apply a rule that
classifies some fraction of that set as lost, on the basis of an assumption about
behaviour in the early period. Report the result as a quantity.

The second step is the entire estimate, and it is a judgement, not an
observation. It cannot be checked against anything, because there is no
independent record of losses to check against. Nobody files a report when a drive
is wiped. The output that would have proved the key was still good stays exactly
where it was, and the estimate cannot be falsified by any future event except one:
the coins move, at which point that particular assumption was wrong and every
figure built on it was too.

This is why this piece does not repeat any of the specific numbers. Not because
the people who produce them are careless, since the better ones state their
assumptions clearly and present ranges. It is because the number detaches from the
method within about one repetition. A carefully hedged range published with its
assumptions becomes a round figure in a summary, and then a fact in a headline,
and the assumption that was doing all the work is gone by the second hop.

## The reading habit this teaches

Generalise it, because this is a pattern rather than a quirk of one topic.

When a figure about crypto arrives without a method, the method is usually the
interesting part and usually the weak part. Ask what was measured and what was
inferred. Ask what would have to be true for the inference to hold. Ask what
observation would prove it wrong, and if the answer is that no observation could,
you are looking at a model output, which may be a good one, but is not a
measurement and should not be repeated as though it were.

The same habit applies to plenty of other categories on this site. Counts of
users, counts of nodes, counts of anything that is actually a count of addresses
or of connections, all have the same structure: a real observable, an assumption
that bridges to the quantity people care about, and a bridge that vanishes when
the number is quoted. The [first transaction on
chain](/articles/the-first-transaction-on-chain/) is a fact you can verify
yourself. The number of people using the system in that period is not, and never
was, however often it gets stated.

## Growth on one side, loss of certainty on the other

What expanded, from the earliest period onward, is the set of outputs that have
never moved. It grows monotonically in one sense, because every day a still
output stays still it enters an older band, and nothing except a spend can remove
it. That accumulating set is the raw material for every claim in this area, and it
is genuine data.

What contracted, at the same time, is anybody's ability to say what that set
means. In the first months there were few enough participants that the difference
between an unmoved output and an abandoned one was partly a matter of who was
around, and the people [running nodes when almost nobody
did](/articles/running-a-node-when-nobody-did/) knew a good deal about each
other. As participation grew, the population behind those outputs became
unknowable, and the interpretive gap between the observable and the interesting
question widened permanently.

Who could tell at the time is the sharpest version of the problem, and the answer
is one person per output, with no way to demonstrate it. A holder knows whether
they still have the key. They cannot prove they do without spending, and spending
destroys the dormancy that made the output interesting in the first place. Proof
of continued control and continued dormancy are mutually exclusive by
construction, which is why this category has stayed unresolvable for its entire
history and why it will stay that way.
